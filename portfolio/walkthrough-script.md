# ClientScope Walkthrough — 3–5 Minutes

## 0:00–0:30 — Problem and entry

Introduce ClientScope as the shared record of client agreement rather than an internal task tracker. Open the clean demo sign-in screen, mention six-hour resets, and enter as Workspace Owner.

## 0:30–1:30 — Agreement and change control

Use My Work to open Harbor Website Launch. Show the approved scope, its numbered history, and the accepted responsive-navigation change linked to the successor agreement.

## 1:30–2:30 — Progress, delivery, and revision

Show mixed milestone progress and the deliverable's first-version feedback, requested revision, and current version awaiting a decision. Briefly point out private provider-backed files and human-governed AI assistance without opening provider consoles.

## 2:30–3:30 — Client authority and preserved history

Sign out, choose Client Approver, open the same project, and inspect or make the pending binding decision. Show actor, role, version, time, and result in Activity. Visit the completed and archived stories to demonstrate read-only history and export.

## 3:30–4:30 — Engineering quality

Use the README architecture diagram to explain the Next.js/Express boundary, MongoDB transactions, contextual authorization, Cloudinary/Gmail/Gemini adapters, exact-image deployment, and CI/browser coverage. Close with the portfolio-scale limitations and the principle that AI never becomes authoritative without a human action.

## Recording privacy check

Record only canonical synthetic data and public repository pages. Exclude secrets, network payloads containing credentials, email recipients, signed URLs, prompts, provider dashboards, CI secret settings, and operational logs. Verify audio, mobile readability, duration, and public unauthenticated playback before publishing the manifest URL.
